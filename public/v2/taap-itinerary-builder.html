Case Study 01

TAAP Itinerary Builder

Giving travel agents the power to curate and share personalized trip itineraries with their clients, across desktop and mobile.

Client
Expedia TAAP
Role
Design Lead
Platform
B2B Desktop & Mobile
Year
2026
Tools
Figma, Figma Make, Miro, Zoom

TAAP is Expedia's affiliate platform for travel agents, giving them access to Expedia's inventory to book trips on behalf of their clients. Despite powerful booking capabilities, agents had no way to bring those bookings together into a cohesive, shareable experience for their travelers.

The Itinerary Builder closes that gap, a dedicated tool to curate, customize, and share branded itineraries across desktop and mobile, complete with hotel details, price breakdowns, notes, and agency information, all in one place. As Design Lead, I owned the look and feel of every screen, from first wireframes to production-ready UI.

The Challenge

Powerful booking tools, no way to share the result.

Agents could book flights, hotels, and experiences through TAAP, but once bookings were made, there was no unified view for the agent or the traveler. Agents manually pieced together itinerary information outside the platform, creating extra work and an inconsistent client experience.

This was also a retention and differentiation challenge. Travel agents are the core users of TAAP, and their satisfaction directly impacts platform loyalty. A branded, professional tool to share with clients, at the desk or on the go, would strengthen the agent–traveler relationship and reflect well on TAAP.

Key constraints
  • ·Saving itineraries mid-session was out of scope for the initial release
  • ·Mobile designs were required from day one
  • ·Accessibility standards had to be met across all screens
  • ·Technical limitations shaped every proposed interaction pattern
The Solution

Led end to end, from discovery through accessible handoff.

I owned the design across desktop and mobile, moving from a competitive audit through a facilitated design sprint, an atomic design system, AI-assisted prototyping, and moderated research before a fully annotated dev handoff.

  1. 01

    Discovery & competitive audit

    I began with a competitive audit and discovery board, reviewing how other platforms handle itinerary sharing and identifying patterns and inspiration across both desktop and mobile form factors.

  2. 02

    A structured 2-day design sprint

    I facilitated a sprint with UX, Research, and Content. Day one reviewed the current experience and presented three interaction concepts; day two was dot voting, discussion, and takeaways.

    • 1Stay on the Trips List: builder opens as an overlay, keeping agents in context
    • 2Open in a new tab: keep the builder open while working elsewhere, mitigating the lack of auto-save
    • 3Inline editing with modals, chosen. Hover to reveal editable sections, click to open contextual modals. Rated the most intuitive.

    Concerns about hover discoverability on mobile and the accessibility of hover-only interactions directly shaped the final direction.

  3. 03

    Wireframes & an atomic design system

    Multiple rounds of wireframes across desktop and mobile, then a comprehensive atomic design system in Figma, tokens for color, typography, spacing, and elevation, with a component library of full variant sets and interactive states. Every component was built to WCAG standards: contrast, focus states, touch targets, and screen-reader considerations, all documented for handoff.

  4. 04

    A working prototype with Figma Make

    To enable realistic testing, I used Figma Make, Figma's AI-powered prototyping tool, to build a functional, interactive prototype of the builder, giving research participants a real product experience rather than a standard click-through.

  5. 05

    Moderated usability research

    A moderated study with 6 TAAP travel agents over Zoom, think-aloud protocol, 8 tasks across the full agent journey. Findings that shaped the final iterations:

    • →The map wasn't considered useful, replaced with a clickable link
    • →Participants wanted more imagery, room photos and destination images
    • →Pre-filled email content needed simplifying
    • →Pre-populated logos were wanted, with the ability to customize
  6. 06

    Accessible, annotated dev handoff

    Final designs shipped as a fully annotated handoff across desktop and mobile, focus order, ARIA labels, color-contrast ratios, keyboard-navigation patterns, and conditional logic for every edge case and interactive state. I also contributed to Expedia Group's AI-consumable design system, helping define token structures and component architecture for rapid AI prototyping.

Results

The core interaction model, validated.

Research validated the model, agents consistently discovered and completed the primary share flow, and the layout was received as clean and professional. Findings and accessibility refinements were incorporated before handoff.

  • ✓ Primary Preview & Share flow consistently discovered and completed by agents
  • ✓ Layout rated clean and professional: positive reception across sessions
  • ✓ Show/hide customization controls: a standout, high-value feature
  • ✓ Figma Make prototype surfaced richer, more nuanced feedback than a click-through
  • ◷ Adoption, share & export metrics: being gathered post-launch, added once available
